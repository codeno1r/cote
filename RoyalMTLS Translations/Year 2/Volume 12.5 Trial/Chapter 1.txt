Chapter 1 - Checking Each Other's Answers

MARCH 14TH. It was dusk right after the end-of-year special exam concluded.

The class representatives were sent back to their respective classes, still unable to sort out their feelings.

Or rather, it might’ve been the participants who were more unsettled than the representatives. They had to start by understanding the full scope of the exam, what happened behind the scenes, and how it was all settled. It wasn't enough to just know who won or lost—they needed to understand the events that had occurred. On their way back to the classroom, where the participants were waiting, it was clear that Horikita and Yōsuke had many questions and doubts about the confrontation between Ichinose and me, but perhaps because Chabashira-sensei was there, they walked in silence all the way to the classroom.

Unable to bear the silence any longer, Chabashira-sensei was the first to speak.

"...Just to clarify, this special exam was quite unique compared to previous years. An important condition was that the information that was given between you representatives and the participants waiting in the classroom was different. The only common understanding was about the class's victory or defeat and the existence of any expulsions. I won't explain this point in detail after we return to the classroom."

"The actual rules... specifically, the rules given to the representatives... it's okay for us to explain them to the participants to clear up any misunderstandings, right?"

"Of course. It's up to you to decide what should be done. We're going to depart soon, but you should discuss and resolve any concerns."

Before touching the classroom door, Chabashira-sensei turned back and advised Horikita and the others.

"Yes, that's the plan. Even the representatives don't know in detail what kind of battles were fought individually. We believe it's necessary to share information."

The representatives didn't know what actions they each took.

Even so, their feelings were focused on understanding the details of the battle between Ichinose and me.

The only thing they knew was that Ichinose had been defeated and Maezono had been expelled.

"You too, Ayanokōji-kun—you'll make time for us, right?"

"Of course. I never intended to end the day with lingering doubts."

With this affirmation, Horikita was satisfied and nodded, and Yōsuke followed suit.

The teacher and the three representatives returned.

The participants who welcomed us back quickly cast curious and varied gazes our way.

When Chabashira-sensei returned the students’ confiscated phones, I quickly skimmed through the messages that arrived as soon as I turned on my phone. After, I placed it in my pocket.

Chabashira-sensei slowly spoke to the still visibly shaken students.

"The end-of-year special exam ended in your victory. With this, Class 2-B has gained a significant amount of class points and… from next month, as third-years, it's almost certain that you'll be promoted to Class A for the first time. However, the situation has also resulted in Maezono being expelled. I find it hard to outright congratulate you in this situation... but still, I want to commend you for fighting well."

In a situation where it was inappropriate to burst into joy, Chabashira-sensei praised the students' efforts while commending them.

However, there was little joy among the classmates.

The reality of their win was still faint, and Maezono had been expelled.

Wildly celebrating now would only invite disapproval.

Everyone was eager to know the details without delay.

"Did Maezono really get expelled?"

Among the hesitant students, Sudō was the first to ask directly.

"Half correct, half incorrect. Indeed, Maezono is processing her expulsion in the staff room, but if you can come up with the necessary private points right here, it's possible to avoid it."

Normally, it wouldn't be strange to confirm one or the other, but Chabashira-sensei must have been aware of the class's total private points. Considering that they likely hadn't reached 20 million, that measure couldn’t be considered. Holding out faint hope for Maezono would only be cruel.

In fact, even if we forcibly tried to save her, we wouldn't be able to make it in time.

We couldn’t stop the expulsion. There was nothing more to do than silently accept it.

"Will I be able to see her if I wait...?"

At this point, Mii-chan voiced her anxiety.

The strong-willed Maezono and the timid Mii-chan. At first glance, they seemed like a mismatch, but surprisingly, the two often hung out together and got along well. It was natural for her not to accept the sudden farewell.

"No... I wonder. At least not right away. She's only just been told about her expulsion and she’s severely shaken. Until that settles, it will be difficult."

At the mention of being shaken, the classmates exchanged complex looks.

"There are many things you might be curious about, but I'm not allowed to speak on the details. If you have questions, it would be good to discuss them later. That's all from me."

Having said that, she quickly ended the conversation, considering the developments that lay ahead.

Horikita obtained permission from Chabashira-sensei and hurriedly took the podium.

"I'm sorry, but could everyone please stay for a while?"

"Are you going to tell us what happened with Maezono-san’s expulsion?"

Nishimura, who had been waiting for this moment, asked Horikita with dissatisfaction.

"Yes. I judged that we can't leave any unresolved issues from this end-of-year special exam. But I can't do it alone. Could I get everyone's cooperation?"

Maezono, who had disappeared from the class.

The cost of victory.

An unclear battle, with its details unknown.

It wouldn't have been strange if at least Kōenji had left his seat, but there was no such sign.

He only moved his gaze to check the situation, staring at himself in a hand mirror.

It seemed he wasn't completely uninterested in the details of the exam, or perhaps he wasn't listening to the teacher or Horikita's conversation. Instead, perhaps he was just absorbed in his own reflection.

It was unclear which it was, but it seemed certain he would stay for a while.

"Including myself, I want to clear up what seems to be our biggest concern first."

After saying that, Horikita began an explanation to eliminate discrepancies between the representatives and participants.

What they each knew and what they didn't.

What the rules were for determining victory, etc.

By reducing discrepancies to some extent, the situation would become clearer.

Yōsuke honestly conveyed that his way of fighting hadn't worked well, and Horikita reflected on how everything had gone smoothly until she was completely defeated by the opposing general, Ichinose.

Then, everyone's attention began to gather on what lay ahead.

That I, the general, had completely shut out Ichinose, who Horikita had described as a formidable opponent.

The process resulted in Maezono’s expulsion, but representatives like Horikita and Yōsuke were also unaware of the circumstances.

First and foremost, Maezono’s case was an unavoidable topic.

"In my opinion... There was definitely a risk of expulsion for the student assigned the role of traitor, but if the rules were properly understood, the risk of expulsion was almost zero. I thought Maezono-san and Ayanokōji-kun understood that well..."

With my name called, most of my classmates turned their gaze to me. Triggered by Horikita's words, I stood up and moved closer to her to explain.

Chabashira-sensei could’ve left, but it seemed she intended to observe the discussion.

"First, I want to apologize for the sudden expulsion of a classmate."

I said that and bowed in apology.

As usual, it was important to proceed with the discussion in an orderly manner.

"The role of the traitor had risks and rewards. If identified as a traitor, one would be expelled, but if not, one could receive either 5 million private points or 50 class points. It didn't matter which Maezono intended to choose; anyone would consider taking the reward home at least once. However, due to the nature of the traitor, the longer they stayed, the more they would end up leaking information to the opposing class. Therefore, getting them to confess early and dealing with them was undoubtedly the right choice for a straightforward victory."

This was the first thing representatives like Horikita and Yōsuke would think of, and participants would easily imagine confessing immediately if they were the traitor.

"Horikita, you’ve fought directly as a representative, so you’d understand. In this special exam, Ichinose was an unimaginably strong opponent. We can agree on that, right?"

"...Yes, she was strong. I couldn't envision any way to win."

Drawing out a clear declaration of defeat again, I nodded.

"I felt the same. Until I faced her, I thought I could manage somehow, but as soon as the one-on-one fight started, I immediately sensed it. For Ichinose, this exam was the best stage to demonstrate her abilities, knowing both her enemies and allies thoroughly. She had the insight to expose both truth and lies."

Horikita nodded with her eyes closed, likely recalling the actual battle.

"There was no chance of winning by fair means. Yet, being appointed as the general, I had to think about how to win. After desperately thinking within the limited time, I came up with only one path to victory."

I paused here, letting everyone digest and absorb my words before continuing.

"The method to win was—to expel the traitor early on."

Naturally, being told this, everyone's line of thought would come to a stop.

‘What is he saying?’ Being confused in such a way would only be expected.

"Expelling Maezono... the traitor, how does that lead to victory?"

Horikita, who was next to me, spouted out the obvious question on everyone’s minds.

"I can't beat Ichinose with a straightforward fight given my abilities. To defeat such an opponent, I thought the only way was a surprise attack. I approached Ichinose early on to negotiate, proposing that we both use up the rights of the traitor. If both parties lose that right, no handicap is created, and no one gets expelled. It should’ve been a welcome development for Ichinose, who believes in winning fairly."

Ichinose disliked sacrificing her comrades more than anyone. No, to be precise, she doesn’t welcome anyone’s expulsion.

That was something everyone had felt over these past two years—no explanation needed.

"My aim was to use the traitor rights to make Ichinose complicit in the expulsion. That was the only method I could think of."

At this point, the classroom became momentarily noisy.

Those who understood the explanation, those who didn’t, and those who fell into both categories.

Only Chabashira-sensei, who had been observing objectively, remained calm.

"Mutually using the rights of the traitor... Indeed, it would be easy to establish with Ichinose-san, and it indirectly implicates her in Maezono-san’s expulsion... That was your aim..."

Horikita, being the representative, naturally imagined the scenario faster than the participants.

"Faced with the reality that she’d unwittingly helped in expelling Maezono, Ichinose was greatly and intensely disturbed. Coupled with her strong feelings of guilt, she couldn't perform to her usual standards and couldn't make satisfactory nominations."

She explained and demonstrated that this was the result.

"Wait a minute, please."

Yōsuke, unable to contain himself, stood up vigorously.

"I also fought as a representative. I wasn't useful, and I understand that I don't have the right to complain or object. But... even so, was it worth sacrificing a classmate? Even if expelling Maezono meant a 100% chance of victory, I can't simply agree, but what if we’d lost? Wasn’t that possibility considered? No matter the representative, I think this method is bordering on overstepping."

"Indeed, that's true. I think Ichinose-san would be disturbed if she knew she helped in Maezono-san's expulsion, but that doesn't guarantee a sure win... right?"

There seemed to be students who agreed. One of them, Ichihashi, muttered this.

"That's right. If you're looking at the situation with just hindsight, then that's all that there is to it. But it was a choice between struggling aimlessly and losing or paying a sacrifice to bet on the chance of winning. Weighing the options, as the class representative and the general, I chose the latter. I thought a defeat here could be fatal. If Class A had won as speculated and our class lost, the gap in class points would’ve been 300. There's no guarantee we could recover in just one year. It's true for any exam, but especially this one—it was a battle we absolutely couldn't afford to lose."

While there were some lies in the reasoning, there was no falsehood in the background leading to Maezono’s expulsion.

To lose without sacrifice, or aim for victory even with the sacrifice.

Fundamentally, it was impossible for anyone to provide a perfect answer.

However, losing would mean a significant setback from the path to Class A; that fact loomed in the background.

"I'm not trying to justify myself, but I wouldn't mind taking a vote here. I believe most of us wouldn’t say it’d be better for us to lose the exam than to sacrifice Maezono."

The classroom fell silent. Students looked at each other or turned away.

It was clear they didn't want to vote.

Most likely, Horikita wouldn't call it either.

It would only cause pain to everyone. The answer was already understood.

As painful as it was, the fact that this class had won was greater than imagined.

A world where Sakayanagi won and Horikita lost.

It was crucial to avoid that. They couldn't go without calculating it in their mind.

Also, the fact that it wasn't themself who was sacrificed was something that couldn't be ignored.

Denying it would require rethinking Sakura's sacrifice.

Still, if there were those who would strongly deny—

"—Isn't that too arrogant? It wasn't right for Ayanokōji-kun alone to decide. Even a representative doesn't have the right to decide if it's okay to sacrifice Maezono."

Thus, students like Yōsuke, who could protect others more than themselves, existed.

Such objections were anticipated.

"That's right. But given the situation where I couldn't consult anyone, I had no choice but to decide alone. And I didn't end up thinking like Yōsuke. I was entrusted with the fate of the class, and I regarded that my top priority was to bring victory to the class."

I confronted Yōsuke's objection head-on with my opinion.

"But... when I think about Maezono, who was randomly chosen—"

"Sorry, but there was a proper reason why it was Maezono."

Why had it been Maezono—eventually, students would’ve voiced their dissatisfaction on this point.

Therefore, I preemptively cut off their words.
